Dexterity Board

Research Assistant | Summer 2025

Jump to 3D Scanner Jump to Robot Grippers

This project focused on designing and building dexterity boards to evaluate end-effector functionality across generalized and nursing-specific tasks. During summer 2025, I worked toward making, and designing my own dexterity board. A dexterity board is a fixture used to determine the capabilities of an end-effector in certain situations.

General Dexterity Board

  • The first project was to build an existing dexterity board from New Dexterity, which determined what end-effectors or bimanual imitation learning devices were most effective in generalized tasks. This includes pick and place, screwing bolts, etc.
  • This board was made of laser-cut acrylic for the main structure, along with 3D printed tasks.
  • One feature of the board is a motor located underneath it, which rotates to simulate complex environments. The design had to be modified in order to accomodate the Dynamixel servo motors the lab had access to.

Nursing Dexterity Board

  • The next project was to create a nursing-specific dexterity board, which included tasks such as peeling Band-aids, opening packaging, and recapping syringes. I collaborated with multiple registered nurses to ensure tasks were representative of what nurses do on a daily basis.
  • One benefit of my design over the general dexterity board is the modularity. Tasks are able to be rearranged due to a having a grid setup.

Quadruped Robot

Leg Design Lead | Summer 2025 - Spring 2026

Jump to Humanoid Robot

Loading full assembly model...

  • Working alongside a group of interdisciplinary engineers, we designed a quadruped robot for the pursuit of research. Many of the design decisions focused on reducing cost, while keeping the robot dextrous.
  • Many challenges came about when dealing with such a low budget. One of which was motor cost, where the Robstride 02 were selected for their general affordability, combined with moderate torque output, good backdrivability, and dual encoder setup.
  • Due to having a motor with limited torque, weight became a critical factor in order for the robot to hold up its own weight with one leg. This required very careful material selection and making difficult trade-offs.

Quadruped Leg

Preparing model views...

  • I was responsible for leading the mechanical design of the quadruped's legs, ensuring they were lightweight, but robust enough to hold up the robot's mass.
  • The design needed to be manufactured in-house, which imposed additional constraints. This was settled through using a combination of FDM printing with PETG for complex geometry, along with 1/8" aluminum plates for rigidity and impact resistance. A carbon fiber tube was used for the "calf," which has great weight and plenty of compressive strength for its use case.
  • A symmetrical four-bar linkage was used to transfer rotation across the leg. This was both cheap, space-efficient, and easy to simulate on MuJoCo.
